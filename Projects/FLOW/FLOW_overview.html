A mental arithmetic block-design paradigm that experimentally induces flow by continuously matching task difficulty to individual skill, contrasted against boredom and overload control conditions.

Background

Flow (Csikszentmihalyi, 1975, 1990, 2000) is the autotelic subjective state that emerges when an individual's skill is matched to the demands of a goal-directed activity with clear and immediate feedback. It is characterised by intense yet effortless concentration, reduced self-referential processing, a sense of control, and intrinsic reward. Despite its theoretical prominence, flow has historically been hard to operationalise in laboratory settings because it requires (a) continuous online adjustment of task difficulty, (b) minimal motor confounds for neuroimaging, and (c) clear goal structure with immediate (implicit) feedback. Ulrich et al. (2014, NeuroImage) introduced the present mental arithmetic paradigm to satisfy all three requirements and reported, with pCASL perfusion fMRI, that flow is associated with increased rCBF in left inferior frontal gyrus and basal ganglia, and decreased rCBF in medial prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and angular gyri — a 'multiple-demand engagement / default-mode disengagement' signature. Ulrich, Keller & Grön (2016b, Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience) replicated and extended these findings with a BOLD block design (30 s blocks), demonstrating a bilateral inverted-U pattern across the multiple-demand network (anterior insula, IFG, pre-SMA, IPL/SPL, thalamus, basal ganglia, midbrain) and a U-pattern in the default-mode network (MPFC, PCC, lateral temporoparietal cortex, hippocampus, amygdala), with concurrent increases in electrodermal activity during flow. Katahira et al. (2018, Frontiers in Psychology) adopted the same paradigm for EEG and showed that flow is characterised by increased frontal theta (cognitive control, immersion) and moderate frontocentral alpha (working-memory load not excessive). Ulrich et al. (2018, Experimental Brain Research) used the paradigm with tDCS over the MPFC and demonstrated baseline-dependent modulation of subjective flow and right-amygdala downregulation. Ulrich et al. (2022, NeuroImage: Reports) reported decisive Bayesian replication evidence (BF > 300) for both invU and U signatures in an independent sample of N = 41; Ulrich, Niemann & Grön (2022, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience) used task-based functional connectivity to show that the right anterior insula serves as a salience-network hub coupling with the central-executive network and decoupling from the ventral striatum during flow. References: Csikszentmihalyi (1975, 1990, 2000); Ulrich et al. (2014) NeuroImage 86:194-202; Ulrich et al. (2016b) SCAN 11:496-507; Katahira et al. (2018) Front Psychol 9:300; Ulrich et al. (2018) Exp Brain Res 236:3223-3237; Ulrich et al. (2022a) NeuroImage Reports 2:100139; Ulrich et al. (2022b) Front Hum Neurosci 16:1067968.
